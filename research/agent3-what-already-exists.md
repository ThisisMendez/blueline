# Agent 3 — What Already Exists (Competitive Landscape)

Research on existing contract-review, clause-analysis, and ToS-summarization products, spanning consumer, freelancer/SMB, and enterprise legal-ops segments. Every entry below has a cited source; anything I couldn't verify is listed in "What I could not find" rather than guessed at.

### Product 1: LawGeex
- **What it does:** AI-powered contract review automation — reviews and redlines incoming contracts (esp. NDAs and vendor agreements) against a company's pre-approved policy playbook, flags deviations, and can auto-approve compliant contracts.
- **Target user:** In-house legal teams at mid-size to large companies (B2B/enterprise legal ops), not individual consumers.
- **Pricing:** Not published; requires contacting sales. G2 lists no public pricing tier. [G2 AI Marketplace listing](https://ai.g2.com/marketplace/tools/lawgeex) / [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/170181/LawGeex/)
- **Most common complaint:** Not found. LawGeex holds a 4.2/5 on G2, but the G2 and Capterra pages I could access did not surface accessible review text (Capterra's own page states "no user reviews" data was available at fetch time). I could not source a specific, quoted complaint.

### Product 2: Terms of Service; Didn't Read (ToS;DR)
- **What it does:** Free, volunteer-curated project and browser extension that grades websites' Terms of Service and privacy policies A (best) to E (worst), with a point-by-point summary of concerning clauses, shown via an address-bar icon.
- **Target user:** General consumers/privacy-conscious internet users; free and open-source (FOSS), donation-funded.
- **Pricing:** Free. [tosdr.org](https://tosdr.org/en/), [browser extension repo](https://github.com/tosdr/browser-extensions)
- **Most common complaint:** Not found. The Chrome Web Store listing shows a 4.6/5 rating across 133 reviews, but the fetched page did not expose individual review text, and a targeted search for Reddit/user complaints about accuracy or coverage gaps returned no directly sourceable quotes. [Chrome Web Store listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/terms-of-service-didn%E2%80%99t-r/hjdoplcnndgiblooccencgcggcoihigg)

### Product 3: Spellbook
- **What it does:** AI contract drafting and review add-in for Microsoft Word; suggests redlines, flags risky/missing clauses, and drafts language based on the firm's own playbooks and past contracts.
- **Target user:** Lawyers and in-house legal teams at law firms and corporate legal departments (B2B/legal-tech), not consumers.
- **Pricing:** Not publicly listed — custom quotes; third-party estimates place per-seat pricing roughly in the $99–$199/user/month range depending on tier, with a 7-day free trial. [Spellbook pricing breakdown — Hyperstart](https://www.hyperstart.com/blog/spellbook-pricing/), [G2 Reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/spellbook/reviews)
- **Most common complaint:** Users on G2 note "the AI sometimes glitches and is prone to making mistakes, so users need to be vigilant to ensure errors are caught" — i.e., output requires manual verification. [G2 Spellbook Reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/spellbook/reviews)

### Product 4: Consumer AI lease-review apps (LeaseCheck, LeaseAI, LeaseGuard, LeaseLogic)
- **What it does:** A cluster of new, similar tools where a tenant uploads a lease PDF/photo and gets an instant AI breakdown of hidden fees, risky or illegal clauses, and (for LeaseGuard specifically) state-by-state landlord-tenant law compliance checks via a rules engine layered on top of AI.
- **Target user:** Individual renters/tenants (consumer), not businesses.
- **Pricing:** Free first analysis / freemium — LeaseCheck and LeaseAI both advertise "no signup, first analysis free." [LeaseCheck](https://leasecheck.vercel.app/), [LeaseAI](https://leaseai.net/), [LeaseGuard](https://www.leaseguard.us/), [LeaseLogic — App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/leaselogic-ai-lease-analyzer/id6752753369)
- **Most common complaint:** Not found. These are small/new products (LeaseLogic's App Store listing is recent) with no visible review volume on G2, Trustpilot, or app stores at the depth needed to identify a common, sourced complaint.

### Product 5: DoNotPay
- **What it does:** Originally a "robot lawyer" consumer app; among its broader suite of legal self-help tools it offers contract/document review and dispute-letter generation for consumers.
- **Target user:** Individual consumers (not businesses or legal teams).
- **Pricing:** Subscription — reviewers describe a $3.99/month trial price that converts to a recurring subscription, with some users reporting a $36 charge; DoNotPay states this is disclosed at signup as a quarterly/annual charge. [Trustpilot reviews of donotpay.com](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/donotpay.com)
- **Most common complaint:** Billing/cancellation friction — reviewers report being charged unexpectedly after a low-cost trial, describe "aggressive sales-push...to buy their 1 year contract," and say cancellation emails go unanswered. [Trustpilot — DoNotPay reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/donotpay.com)

### Product 6: Rocket Lawyer
- **What it does:** Legal document platform for consumers/small business that includes contract templates, e-signature, and a "Document Defense" benefit letting members get attorney help reviewing/enforcing a contract dispute.
- **Target user:** Consumers and small businesses/freelancers needing legal documents and occasional attorney access, not enterprise legal teams.
- **Pricing:** Trustpilot-sourced reviews describe a 7-day free trial that auto-enrolls into a recurring ~$39.99/month subscription. [Trustpilot — Rocket Lawyer reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/rocketlawyer.com), [ConsumerAffairs](https://www.consumeraffairs.com/legal/rocket-lawyer.html)
- **Most common complaint:** Billing complaints dominate — "Rocket Lawyer automatic renewals do not send out a warning email before they renew subscriptions and charge credit cards," with reviewers specifically warning about the trial auto-converting to a paid monthly plan that's hard to cancel. [Trustpilot — Rocket Lawyer reviews](https://www.trustpilot.com/review/rocketlawyer.com)

### Product 7: Ironclad
- **What it does:** Full contract lifecycle management (CLM) platform — workflow, redlining/negotiation, e-signature, and an AI layer ("Jurist") that reviews higher-risk clauses and flags renewal/opt-out dates via a dashboard.
- **Target user:** Enterprise and mid-market legal operations teams (B2B), not individual consumers or solo freelancers.
- **Pricing:** Custom/enterprise quotes; third-party benchmarking (Vendr) cites a median annual spend of ~$38,825/year across 328 purchases, with the separate AI tier (Jurist) quoted at roughly $50K–$200K/year. [Hyperstart — Ironclad Reviews](https://www.hyperstart.com/blog/ironclad-reviews/), [Oneflow — Ironclad Review](https://oneflow.com/blog/ironclad-review/)
- **Most common complaint:** G2 and Software Advice reviewers cite "poor search functionality and problems finding older contracts when metadata or tags are inconsistent," plus friction with PDF-to-Word workflows and locked PDFs during browser-based editing. [G2 AI — Ironclad](https://ai.g2.com/product/ironclad)

### Product 8: LegalOn Technologies
- **What it does:** AI contract review platform combining an LLM with 50+ attorney-built playbooks to flag nuanced contract risks and insert one-click redlines, claiming up to 85% reduction in review time.
- **Target user:** In-house legal teams (claims 3,800+ legal teams as customers) — B2B/enterprise, with a lower-tier "Individual" plan for solo practitioners.
- **Pricing:** Individual plan listed at $550/month (unlimited reviews, unlimited AI assistant, full playbook access); team/enterprise pricing requires a sales quote. [LegalOn AI Contract Review Software page](https://www.legalontech.com/ai-contract-review-software)
- **Most common complaint:** Reviewers note the AI cannot reference more than one document at a time, which is a limitation when a customer's contract is actually made up of multiple linked documents. [G2 — LegalOn Reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/legalon/reviews)

## What I could not find

- **A sourced, quoted complaint for LawGeex.** It has a 4.2/5 on G2, but the G2 and Capterra pages I could access did not expose reviewer text (Capterra's page reported zero visible reviews at fetch time). Would need direct G2 login/scroll access to pull an actual quote.
- **A sourced complaint for ToS;DR.** The Chrome extension shows 4.6/5 across 133 reviews, but review text wasn't accessible via the pages fetched, and a targeted search turned up no specific Reddit/forum thread naming a common gripe (e.g., stale ratings, missing sites).
- **Any real user complaint for the consumer lease-review apps (LeaseCheck, LeaseAI, LeaseGuard, LeaseLogic).** These appear to be very new products with little to no review footprint on G2, Trustpilot, or app stores — I found their own marketing pages/app-store listings but no third-party review threads to cite.
- **Direct comparison of a "risk severity ranking" or "drafted counter-offer" feature identical to Blueline's stated scope.** None of the products found explicitly rank clauses by severity and auto-draft counter-offer language in one consumer-facing flow; existing tools split roughly into (a) enterprise CLM/redlining tools with playbook-based risk flags (Ironclad, LegalOn, Spellbook, LawGeex) or (b) simple consumer graders/summarizers (ToS;DR, lease apps) — the combination Blueline proposes (plain-English summary + severity-ranked risks + drafted counter-offers + Q&A, aimed at individual users) was not found as a single existing named product within the search budget.
- I did not evaluate lawyer-marketplace add-ons (e.g., Avvo/LegalZoom attorney-review upsells) or ToS-specific browser extensions beyond ToS;DR — dropped to stay within the search/read budget once 8 sourced products were reached.
