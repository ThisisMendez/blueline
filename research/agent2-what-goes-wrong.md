# Agent 2 — What Goes Wrong (Clause-Level Evidence of Real Harm)

Research method: 10 web searches, no page fetches beyond search-result summaries (stayed under the 12 search / 15 read cap). Evidence drawn from FTC/CFPB actions, law-firm litigation write-ups, class-action trackers, and case law summaries. Every finding below has a source URL; anything I couldn't source is listed at the bottom instead of guessed at.

## Ranked clause types

1. **Auto-renewal / hard-to-cancel subscription terms** — Highest volume of regulator and legal-press evidence: sustained FTC rulemaking (Click-to-Cancel), an active FTC v. Fitness International complaint, and recurring class actions under ROSCA. This is the single most litigated/regulated clause type in the consumer contract space right now.
2. **Forced arbitration / class-action waiver clauses** — Extremely well documented via CFPB rulemaking and multiple advocacy/legal orgs (Consumer Reports, Center for American Progress, Center for Justice & Democracy) with hard statistics on outcomes. Ranked high because the harm is quantified (win-rate data), not just anecdotal.
3. **Non-compete clauses** — Backed by a full federal rulemaking (FTC non-compete ban) plus a specific FTC enforcement action against a company for using non-competes on low-wage workers. Strong evidence of concrete harm to career/finances, specifically called out by Congress members using worker testimony.
4. **Early termination fees / penalty clauses** — Multiple class-action lawsuits with settlement dollar amounts (Verizon $21M settlement), decided case law (California Cellphone Termination Fee Cases), and an active consumer dispute ecosystem (FairShake, demand-letter templates). Recurring and quantifiable.
5. **Liability caps / limitation of liability** — Widely flagged by contract-law practitioners as one of the two most-disputed clauses when a SaaS relationship breaks down, with a concrete illustrative harm pattern (12-month-fee cap vs. multi-million-dollar breach). Evidence is more "practitioner consensus" than a filed lawsuit, so ranked below items with regulatory/case-law backing.
6. **IP assignment / work-for-hire ambiguity** — Backed by an actual appellate case (Playboy Enterprises v. Dumas) showing real freelancer/company disputes over ownership, plus repeated legal-blog warnings that freelance "work for hire" clauses are often unenforceable yet still trigger disputes. Ranked here because evidence is mostly one landmark case plus advisory commentary rather than a stream of ongoing litigation.
7. **Security deposit forfeiture / withholding** — Well documented via state penalty statutes (treble damages in some states) and small-claims guidance, but evidence here is mostly about tenant remedies rather than sourced accounts of specific large disputes.
8. **Indemnification clauses** — Consistently flagged by business-law firms as a clause that can "sink a small business," but I could not find a specific sourced lawsuit or news account of a real business harmed this way — only practitioner warnings. Ranked lowest of the well-evidenced categories for that reason.
9. **Rent escalation / unilateral rent increase clauses** — Some evidence of disputes (ambiguous CPI-based escalation clauses causing commercial lease conflicts) but sourcing was general legal-guide material, not a specific documented case.
10. **Unilateral termination clauses (landlord-tenant)** — Found process/procedure information (notice requirements, eviction lawsuits) but no sourced account of a specific dispute where a unilateral termination clause itself (as opposed to normal lease enforcement) caused documented harm.

## Evidence

### Finding 1
- **Clause type:** Auto-renewal / cancellation friction
- **What happened:** The FTC filed a complaint against Fitness International and Fitness & Sports Clubs (LA Fitness) alleging members could cancel only by visiting a gym in person during limited hours or mailing a cancellation form by certified mail at their own expense; forms submitted online logins were mishandled and mailed forms were often ignored, resulting in continued billing after cancellation attempts.
- **Source:** https://advertisinglaw.fkks.com/post/102mrd9/la-fitness-and-the-ftc-square-off-over-gym-membership-cancellation

### Finding 2
- **Clause type:** Auto-renewal (regulatory response)
- **What happened:** The FTC's 2024 "Click-to-Cancel" rule (requiring cancellation to be as easy as sign-up) was vacated by the Eighth Circuit in July 2025 on procedural grounds; the FTC relies on ROSCA and FTC Act Section 5 to keep bringing enforcement actions against companies with deceptive auto-renewal/cancellation practices, and reopened rulemaking in March 2026.
- **Source:** https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2025/09/ftc-steps-up-subscription-enforcement-after-click-to-cancel-rule

### Finding 3
- **Clause type:** Forced arbitration / class-action waiver
- **What happened:** Analysis of arbitration case outcomes found corporations won roughly 93% of consumer arbitration disputes; in a sample of 1,060 arbitration cases (2010–2011), consumers won only 32 cases and obtained relief in 46 more (a combined ~7% success rate), recovering about $400,000 total while arbitrators ordered consumers to pay companies $2.8 million.
- **Source:** https://www.consumerreports.org/mandatory-binding-arbitration/forced-arbitration-clause-for-concern/

### Finding 4
- **Clause type:** Forced arbitration / class-action waiver
- **What happened:** The CFPB found mandatory arbitration clauses with class-action bans made it "nearly impossible" for groups of consumers harmed by the same practice to hold a company accountable, and issued a rule (later repealed by Congress) intended to stop companies using arbitration clauses to block group claims.
- **Source:** https://www.consumerfinance.gov/archive/newsroom/cfpb-issues-rule-ban-companies-using-arbitration-clauses-deny-groups-people-their-day-court/

### Finding 5
- **Clause type:** Non-compete clauses
- **What happened:** Prior to its 2024 rulemaking, the FTC brought an enforcement action against a Michigan-based security guard company and its executives for imposing coercive non-compete agreements on low-wage employees. Worker testimony cited in the rulemaking record described non-competes blocking people from taking better jobs or starting businesses, damaging their finances and careers.
- **Source:** https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/04/ftc-announces-rule-banning-noncompetes

### Finding 6
- **Clause type:** Early termination / penalty fees
- **What happened:** Verizon Wireless agreed to a $21 million settlement in a California class action over early termination fees; separately, a 2007 class action (Waudby v. Verizon) alleged Verizon disguised an equipment-cost recovery fee as a liquidated-damages clause that was an illegal penalty, and earlier (2003) suits against Sprint Spectrum alleged ETFs violated California Civil Code §1671(d).
- **Source:** https://www.bigclassaction.com/lawsuit/cell_phone_early_termination_fees.php

### Finding 7
- **Clause type:** IP assignment / work-for-hire
- **What happened:** In Playboy Enterprises v. Dumas, the Second Circuit reviewed 285 artworks by freelance illustrator Patrick Nagel; because the written agreements for works created 1978–1979 did not sufficiently establish "work for hire" status under the 1976 Copyright Act, the court held those works were NOT work-for-hire, meaning copyright belonged to Nagel's estate rather than Playboy — despite Playboy having commissioned and paid for the work.
- **Source:** https://patentpc.com/blog/how-to-prevent-ip-ownership-disputes-with-contractors-and-freelancers

### Finding 8
- **Clause type:** Liability caps / limitation of liability
- **What happened:** Contract practitioners note that limitation-of-liability and termination-rights clauses are the two most frequently disputed provisions when a SaaS vendor relationship breaks down; a common structure caps vendor liability at trailing-12-months fees (e.g., $100,000), meaning a customer facing a $5 million loss from a data breach could be left covering $4.9 million themselves unless they negotiated carve-outs for gross negligence or security failures.
- **Source:** https://www.cloudnuro.ai/blog/saas-liability-cap

### Finding 9
- **Clause type:** Security deposit forfeiture
- **What happened:** In multiple states, landlords who wrongfully withhold a security deposit (failing to return it or provide an itemized damage list within the statutory window, e.g. 30 days) forfeit their right to withhold any amount and can be held liable for two to three times the deposit amount plus attorney fees — a penalty structure that exists specifically because this dispute pattern is common enough to legislate against.
- **Source:** https://www.legalmatch.com/law-library/article/wrongful-withholding-of-a-security-deposit.html

## What I could not find
- A specific, named, sourced lawsuit or news account (as opposed to general practitioner warnings) where an **indemnification clause** in a small-business contract caused a documented real-world loss. Multiple law firms describe indemnification as a clause that can "sink a small business," but none of the search results pointed to a concrete case with named parties and outcome.
- A sourced account of a **unilateral termination clause** (landlord or vendor) itself — as distinct from routine lease-violation eviction proceedings — being the specific trigger of a documented dispute or lawsuit.
- Concrete, named case evidence for **rent escalation clause** disputes; only general legal-guide material on CPI-based escalation ambiguity was found, not a specific reported dispute.
- Any evidence (found or not found) on **data/privacy assignment clauses** or **exclusivity clauses** as recurring pain points — these were not covered by the searches run within the budget and should be treated as an open gap, not as "low risk."
- Fee-escalator clauses in the sense of recurring price-increase clauses in SaaS/vendor contracts (as opposed to cell-phone ETFs and rent escalation) — not directly evidenced within the search budget.
