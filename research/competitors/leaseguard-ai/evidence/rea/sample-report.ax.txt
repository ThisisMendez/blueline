    - text: HIGH RISK Deposit Deductions Without Written Notice May Not Be Enforceable
    - paragraph: "\"Landlord may deduct from the security deposit for any damages at Landlord's sole discretion, without itemization.\""
    - paragraph: Why It Matters
    - paragraph: Florida Statute 83.49(3) sets out a written, itemized claim process (by certified mail, within 30 days) before a landlord may keep any part of your deposit. A clause allowing undocumented deductions at the landlord's discretion may conflict with that process and may not be enforceable.
    - paragraph: Recommended Action
    - paragraph: Ask for this clause to be revised to follow the FS 83.49(3) written-notice process. If the landlord refuses, a tenant-rights attorney or your county's legal aid office can tell you how the statute applies.
    - text: ⚖ F.S. 83.49(3) MEDIUM RISK Late Fee That May Exceed Florida's Cap
    - paragraph: "\"A late fee equal to 25% of monthly rent will be charged if rent is not received by the 2nd of the month.\""
    - paragraph: Why It Matters
    - paragraph: Florida's 2026 late-fee framework (FS 83.808) caps late fees at the greater of $20 or 20% of monthly rent. A 25% fee appears to exceed that cap, and the 1-day grace period is shorter than what most Florida leases provide.
    - paragraph: Recommended Action
    - paragraph: "Request the fee be brought within the FS 83.808 cap and ask for at least a 3–5 day grace period. Template: 'I'd like the late fee revised to align with Florida's statutory cap under FS 83.808.'"
    - text: ⚖ F.S. 83.808 MEDIUM RISK Vague Landlord Entry Language
    - paragraph: "\"Landlord may enter the premises upon reasonable notice for inspections or repairs.\""
    - paragraph: Why It Matters
    - paragraph: Florida Statute 83.53 generally requires at least 12 hours' notice before non-emergency entry. "Reasonable notice" with no defined timeframe leaves that open to interpretation — and the ambiguity tends to favor the landlord.
    - paragraph: Recommended Action
    - paragraph: Ask for the clause to specify 'at least 12 hours' advance notice, during reasonable hours, except in emergencies' — matching F.S. 83.53.
    - text: ⚖ F.S. 83.53 MEDIUM RISK One-Sided Attorney Fees Clause
    - paragraph: "\"In any dispute, Tenant shall pay all of Landlord's attorney fees and legal costs.\""
    - paragraph: Why It Matters
    - paragraph: Florida Statute 83.48 generally makes attorney-fee rights mutual in residential lease disputes — the prevailing party may recover fees. One-sided language may not operate the way it reads, but it can pressure tenants who don't know the statute.
    - paragraph: Recommended Action
    - paragraph: "Request mutual language: 'The prevailing party in any dispute shall be entitled to reasonable attorney's fees.' Cite F.S. 83.48."
    - text: ⚖ F.S. 83.48 MEDIUM RISK Auto-Renewal With a 90-Day Opt-Out Window
    - paragraph: "\"This lease shall automatically renew for a 12-month term unless Tenant provides written notice at least 90 days prior to expiration.\""
    - paragraph: Why It Matters
    - paragraph: A 90-day opt-out window is unusually long and easy to miss — locking you into another full year. Florida law does not prohibit auto-renewal, which makes the notice window itself the negotiation point.
    - paragraph: Recommended Action
    - paragraph: Ask to shorten the notice window to 30–60 days, make it mutual, or convert to month-to-month at expiration. Put a calendar reminder on the deadline either way.
