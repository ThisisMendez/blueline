# Reviewer guide: is a flag worth showing?

For anyone labelling Blueline flags by hand: the owner now, and the independent reviewers ticket 09 needs later. It covers one question, whether a flagged clause costs the reader enough to be worth a warning. The examples are invented. None comes from the gold set or the labelled flags.

## Judge four things separately

1. **Accuracy.** Does the clause say what the flag says it does?
2. **Materiality.** Does it create a meaningful risk, beyond an ordinary payment?
3. **Severity.** If it does, is high, medium or low right? PRD.md's "My red lines" sets the bands.
4. **Scope.** Is the document a residential lease packet Blueline is meant to review?

A flag can be accurate and still not material. A material flag can still carry the wrong severity. Keep the four answers apart.

## Ordinary payment or meaningful risk?

An **ordinary payment** is the price of what the reader signed up for, paid on the terms they would expect. Having to pay it, or not getting it back for a period already used, isn't by itself a reason to flag.

A **meaningful risk** creates at least one of these:

- **an additional obligation:** a duty to pay or do something beyond the basic deal
- **a penalty:** a charge for leaving, changing, or missing a step
- **a loss of rights:** a remedy, refund, option or protection the reader would otherwise have
- **significant financial exposure:** an amount that is large, open-ended, or out of proportion to what was used

Passing every clause that involves money makes the rule too broad to fail anything.

### Examples

| Clause | Ordinary or meaningful | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A monthly subscription is billed at the start of each month, and a cancellation stops the next charge but doesn't refund the current month. | Ordinary | The reader pays for a month they can still use. Nothing extra is owed. |
| Cancelling before the end of a 12-month plan costs a fee equal to all the remaining months. | Meaningful: penalty, significant exposure | Leaving early costs far more than the service used. |
| Rent is due on the first of each month. | Ordinary | It's the basic deal. |
| A tenant who ends the lease early owes two months' rent as a break fee, plus rent until the unit is re-let. | Meaningful: penalty, open-ended exposure | The amount depends on events the tenant can't control. |
| A one-off setup fee is stated up front and charged once. | Ordinary | Known in advance and bounded. |
| The landlord may keep the whole deposit for "any cleaning required", with no itemised list. | Meaningful: loss of rights, significant exposure | It can cost most or all of the deposit with no way to check the deductions. |

## The triggering condition matters

A cost that only arrives when something happens is judged by that event. Ask whether the event is plausible for an ordinary reader, and whether the text makes clear what sets it off. A severe consequence behind a remote trigger, or a mild one behind a likely trigger, can both be right to flag. Name which it is.

## What a FAIL looks like

FAIL a flag when the clause:

- describes an ordinary payment, with no penalty, extra obligation, lost right or significant exposure
- is unusual or oddly worded but costs nothing
- states a protection for the reader, or a duty on the other side
- doesn't say what the flag claims it says
