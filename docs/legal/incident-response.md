# Incident response

1. Alexis Mendez owns triage. Record the UTC time, how the incident was found, affected components and the evidence location. Keep lease content, secrets, and user lists in a restricted location.
2. Contain promptly. Disable affected integrations where possible, revoke or rotate exposed credentials, and restrict access. Preserve the evidence needed to establish what happened.
3. Identify types of data, scope, affected users and locations, and the earliest legal or contractual deadline. Contact the affected vendor. Obtain counsel input on notification and preservation duties. Track deadlines from discovery and confirmation separately.
4. Give affected people any required notice without unreasonable delay. Under Rhode Island's breach statute, some qualifying private-sector incidents require notice no later than 45 calendar days after confirmation and the ability to ascertain the required information. Affected Rhode Island residents, the attorney general, and major credit reporting agencies may need notice depending on scope. Check the current text, exceptions, required content, and any shorter deadline that applies. [R.I. Gen. Laws § 11-49.3-4](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE11/11-49.3/11-49.3-4.htm)
5. Verify recovery and access controls, follow up with affected people as appropriate, and update the private compliance record with cause, fixes and review dates. Rehearse the response on a synthetic incident and update the procedure after material infrastructure changes.

Rhode Island's information security provisions address the sensitive information a business holds and how it protects and disposes of it. [R.I. Gen. Laws § 11-49.3-2](https://webserver.rilegislature.gov/Statutes/TITLE11/11-49.3/11-49.3-2.htm)
