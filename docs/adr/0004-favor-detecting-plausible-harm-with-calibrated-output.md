# 0004. Favor detecting plausible harm with calibrated output

When a clause has a plausible, material downside for a lease signer, Blueline favors flagging it over missing a serious harm, even at the cost of some harmless warnings. Each flag still needs the exact source sentence and an explanation of the scenario that would make the downside matter; unusual wording alone does not qualify.

When the outcome is uncertain, Blueline states the concrete concern and its condition rather than offering a vague hedge or declaring the consequence certain. When a complete agreement yields no material flags, it says “No material flags found in the text reviewed” and gives a neutral summary. It does not manufacture warnings or call the lease safe to sign. This approach can create extra anxiety from false alarms and make a clean review feel less valuable, but it keeps the answer tied to what the document supports.
